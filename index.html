About

I’m a bioinformatics analyst and biostatistician based in Lexington, Kentucky. I enjoy working with data from biology, medicine, and public health, and helping researchers find clear answers in it.

At the Markey Cancer Center, I support the whole research process, from planning a study to analyzing the data and explaining what it means. I work closely with lab scientists and clinicians so they can publish their findings and win funding for their next projects.

A big part of my work is helping teams put together competitive grant applications. I write the statistical sections, run power and sample size calculations, design the analysis plans, and prepare the figures and supplementary analyses that make a proposal strong.

I work with many kinds of genomic data, from bulk and single cell RNA-seq to spatial transcriptomics, ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, proteomics, metabolomics, and microbiome data. I also draw on large public resources such as TCGA, GDC, cBioPortal, GEO, and GTEx. Before Kentucky, I was a biostatistician at LSU Health Sciences Center, where I ran the statistics for clinical trials and studied national hospital admission data.

Right now I’m pursuing a PhD in Epidemiology and Biostatistics along with an MBA at the University of Kentucky. This builds on my earlier graduate work in Mathematics (Operations Research and Statistics) and Applied Statistics.
